LIQUEUR WINE

Concise textual description

The visual and aromatic characteristics correspond to the different types of wine, white, rosé
or red, depending on the grape variety used. Sweet, with body, unctuous, well-balanced, with
a very long finish.

None of their characteristics suggest oxidation, except those resulting from proper ageing, in
which case the aroma and taste characteristics inherent in the maturing process are prevalent.
There are no organoleptic defects in the aroma, flavour or colour.

4. WINE-MAKING PRACTICES

a. Essential oenological practices

Type of oenological practice:               Specific oenological practice
Description of practice:
Minimum natural alcoholic strength:
-      White grapes used to make white wines: 10% by volume
-      Red grapes used to make rosé wines: 11 % by volume
-      Red grapes used to make red wines: 12 % by volume
-      Red grapes used to make liqueur wines: 12 % by volume


Type of oenological practice:               Relevant restriction on making the wines
Description of practice:
- For the extraction of must from fresh grapes in winemaking involving fermentation off
skins, or of wine from fermented grape marc in red winemaking, the only mechanised
systems permitted are those that do not damage or tear the solid parts of bunches. Use of
high-speed centrifugal crushers is prohibited.
-       Use of continuous presses is prohibited.
-       In the extraction of must or wine and its separation from the grape marc, the yield
must not exceed 70 litres of must or wine per 100 kilograms of grapes. In no case may
batches of must or wine obtained by applying unsuitable pressure be used to make protected
wines.




Type of oenological practice:                            Cultural practice
Description of practice:
Irrigation is regulated annually by the managing body in order to maintain the balance
between the growth potential of the plant and the climate/soil eco-system. The methods are
drip and flood irrigation, or sprinklers.

Where plantations are on land where irrigation cannot be used, planting density is a minimum
of 1 100 plants per hectare. Vines can be gobelet-trained or trellised.

Where plantations are on land which can be irrigated, planting density is a minimum of 1 600
plants per hectare.

Maximum yields on non-irrigated land: 4 500 kilograms per hectare for white wines (31.5
hectolitres per hectare); 4 000 kilograms per hectare for red wines (28 hectolitres per
hectare);

Maximum yields on land which can be irrigated: 9 000 kilgrams per hectare for white wines
(63 hectolitres per hectare); 7 000 kilograms per hectare for red wines (49 hectolitres per
hectare);

5. DEMARCATED AREA

The production area of the wines covered by the designation of origin is formed of the areas
of land included in the wine register, all located within the municipality of Yecla in the
region of Murcia.

The processing area matches the production and ageing areas.

7. DESCRIPTION OF THE LINK(S)

Labd.newWineName.singLeDocumentLinkWithArea.conciseDetails

Yecla has a dry continental climate with hot summers and very cold winters. Average annual
rainfall is 300 millimetres. The annual sunshine exceeds 3 000 hours.

The most important landscape characteristic in this area is the unusual elevation of the
plateau, which ranges from 535 to 700 metres.

The endorheic nature of the river basins in the area is notable characteristic of the water
system.

The soils are, among other things, calcareous and deep with good permeability and especially
poor in organic matter. There is archaeological evidence of vine-growing since the first
century BC.

8. ESSENTIAL FURTHER CONDITIONS

Legal framework:                         In national legislation
Type of further condition:               Additional provisions relating to labelling
Description of the condition:
The following must appear on the labels of the packaged wines: ‘Denominación de Origen
Yecla’ or ‘Denominación de Origen Protegida Yecla’. The labels are approved by the
inspection body that verifies the requirements of this product specification.

The packaging in which the wine is dispatched must include quality seals issued by the
control body. These are single-use and must be attached in the winery.

The following may be used: Noble, Añejo, Viejo, Crianza, Reserva and Gran reserva.
