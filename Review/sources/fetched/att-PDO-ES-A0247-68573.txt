4. WINE-MAKING PRACTICES

       a. Essential oenological practices

Type of oenological practice: Cultural practice

Description of practice:
The planting density must be between a minimum of 1 300 plants per hectare and a maximum
of 4 500 plants per hectare, distributed evenly over the whole vine-planting area.

Type of oenological practice: Specific oenological practice
                                                               Ref. Ares (2014)2153914 – 30/06/2014




Description of practice:
During the harvest, only healthy batches of grape that have reached a suitable stage of
ripeness and have an alcohol content of at least 170 grams per litre of must are sent to be
made into wines covered by the PDO. Any grapes that are not in perfect condition must be set
apart.

Sufficient pressure must be applied to extract the must or wine and separate it from the marc,
so that the yield does not exceed 70 litres of wine for each 100 kilograms of grapes.



Type of oenological practice: Relevant restriction on making the wines

Description of practice:
It is not permitted to use techniques that involve pre-heating the grapes or heating the musts
or wines in the presence of the marc in order to force colour extraction.

7. DESCRIPTION OF THE LINK(S)

Label.newWineName.singLeDocument. LinkWithArea.conciseDetails

The link between these wines and their geographical area dates from the 2nd century BC.
Their fine quality has been spoken of down the centuries. The climate conditions, altitude and
great variety of soils give the wines great aromatic richness, structure and mineral
characteristics. The difference in temperatures between day and night during ripening give
rise to aroma precursors in the grapes. The slow ripening of the grapes develops phenolic
compounds that bring colour, structure, elegance and longevity to the wines. The vineyards
located at altitude are exposed to high levels of sunshine. As a result, the grapes have a
thicker skin than is usual, giving the wines higher levels of anthocyanins and polyphenols.



8. ESSENTIAL FURTHER CONDITIONS

Legal framework: In national legislation

Type of further condition: Packaging within the demarcated area

Description of the condition:

Packaging must take place in the demarcated geographical area stated in the product
specification. There has always been a rule that the wines must be bottled in the wineries
within the production area, as successive published orders have shown.

The main reason for this is to maintain the quality and unique nature of the wine, and to
prevent oxidation-reduction phenomena during transportation.

Legal framework: In national legislation

Type of further condition: Additional provisions relating to labelling

Description of the condition:

Labels must bear the following wording: Denominación de Origen ‘Calatayud’.

Products intended for consumption must bear guarantee marks, numbered and issued by the
Regulatory Board, to be attached in the registered winery, in a way that ensures that they
cannot be re-used.

For the ‘Calatayud’ Superior wines, a specific label and secondary label must be designed,
clearly stating ‘Calatayud Superior’.
