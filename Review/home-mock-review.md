# Home page mock (Salon book, v3): first-principles review

18 Oct 2026. Reviewed from fresh screenshots at 1440 px and 390 px.

**Outcome (owner):** map as hero (card 11:39Z), source logos kept, not names (11:47Z), soft buttons (11:49Z). Built as `Review/mock-home-v1/`.

**The page has one job.** A WSET or Wine Scholar student opens a link. Within five seconds they should know what this is and believe it is
serious, and then click "Open the atlas". Everything below is judged against that job.

## Verdict
The typography and restraint are right, and the page reads premium. The structure is wrong, though: it **tells** instead of **shows**.
The product is a beautiful satellite atlas, and nobody sees it until the last screen. You are right about the photo, and the photo is a
symptom of that deeper problem.

## What's bad, worst first
1. **The hero photo.** It's a phone snapshot with a flat, washed-out sky, power lines, pylons and a lorry on the road, with a blurry vine
   leaf in front. It says "the edge of a town in Spain", not wine. Even a perfect vineyard photo would be the wrong hero, because every
   wine blog, tour operator and bottle shop opens with one. It doesn't sell an *atlas*. [V: screenshot]
2. **Nothing above the fold shows the product.** Someone reading the first screen gets a headline, a paragraph and two buttons. The map
   only appears in the band near the bottom, and it is the most distinctive thing on the page.
3. **The logo strip.** It looks rough: low-resolution grey smudges, with Spain's coat of arms shrunk to a blob. It is also legally shaky
   (the coat of arms, the EU emblem implying endorsement, and the trademarks). On top of that, it contradicts itself, because Wikimedia
   Commons is not an "official source". It's where the photos come from. I recommend typeset names and dropping Commons from the strip.
4. **The three pillars** (icon, bold line, two lines of grey text) are the most template-looking part of the page. They are pure SaaS
   boilerplate, and the line icons add nothing. Three real product views (the map, a guide, a study question) would make the same
   claims and prove them.
5. **The guide cards sell thinness.** There are three cards with the same title pattern, two of them say "1-minute read", and all three
   are Rioja. A student concludes "there's one region". That is honest (Terroir written, 4 of 79 [R]), but there's no need to lead with it.
   Show one strong Rioja guide instead of three weak cards.
6. **"Exam-style questions" is not true yet** [A]. Study today is find-it-on-the-map rounds and four-choice questions, not exam-format
   questions. WSET students will notice. Either reword it to "study rounds on the map that make it stick" or make it true first.
7. **"Open the atlas" appears three times** (top bar, hero and map band), and "Atlas" is in the menu as well. The bar button next to the
   "Atlas" item is redundant. Keep the menu item and drop the bar button on the home page.
8. **The best selling line is buried.** "Free, in your browser, no account" is the strongest reason to click, and it sits at the bottom.
   It belongs right under the hero buttons.
9. **There's no proof.** There is no number and no "who made this". A coverage line ("Every DO and Vino de Pago in Spain, mapped") is
   proof we already have [R, to verify the count]. Who made it and why is your call. I won't invent it.
10. **Phone.** The first screen is all text. The photo then shrinks to a thin strip with a caption that wraps awkwardly. The logos fall
    into a ragged 2×2 grid, and the outlined "Menu" button is heavy.

## What's good, keep it
- **The headline type.** Newsreader at display size gives exactly the salon-book feel. "Know the land behind the glass." works.
- **Restraint.** There's a lot of white space, one accent, black and champagne consistent with the app, and soft 6 px buttons that look right.
- **The sub-line.** The audience is named in the first five words.
- **The map band.** It is the only visual that only AtlaSlope could have. It should be the hero, not the footer.
- **The beehive hut photo** (guarda viñas). It is distinctive and specific to Rioja, and a good guide image.
- **Top bar and footer.** They are clean and match the agreed site map.

## What I'd change (less is more: five blocks instead of seven)
1. **Bar:** logo · Atlas · Study · Guides · About · Search. There is no duplicate CTA button.
2. **Hero:** the headline and sub-line sit on the left, and the product sits on the right or full-bleed behind them. The product is our
   own satellite map of northern Spain, with the DO outlines in champagne and Rioja selected. Both buttons sit under the sub-line,
   followed by "Free, in your browser, no account."
3. **Sources line:** "Built on official sources: EU single documents · Pliegos de condiciones · MAPA · IGN España", typeset with no logos.
4. **Show, don't tell:** three real product crops replace the pillars: the map, a guide page with a "fly there" link, and a study round.
   Each gets one caption line.
5. **One featured guide:** Rioja, with the hut photo, a pull quote from its Terroir section and "Read the guide". The other cards come back when more regions are written.
6. **Footer** as is. The closing map band goes, because the map is now the hero.

## Hero options (card in the thread)
- **Map as hero** (recommended): it's unique, it's free (our own imagery and data), it shows the product, and it has no photo-quality problem.
- **Better photo:** a hand-picked high-resolution Commons original (Rioja Alavesa under the Sierra de Cantabria, or the Priorat terraces).
  It looks prettier but generic, it needs credits, and Commons rate-limits our downloads.
- **Photo plus product:** a photo backdrop with a framed map screenshot on top. It's the common SaaS pattern and busier, which works against "less is more".
