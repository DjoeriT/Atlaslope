Rioja

A 100 km trough along the Ebro, walled by the Sierra de Cantabria to the north and the Sierra de la Demanda to the south. Three zones, three climates, one grape above all.

Where it sits

The vineyards follow the Ebro from Haro down to Alfaro. The northern wall stops Atlantic cloud, so the valley is drier and sunnier than Bilbao, 80 km away.

Why it tastes as it does

West to east, cool to warm. Rioja Alta and Alavesa sit at 500 to 700 m under Atlantic influence: Tempranillo ripens slowly and keeps its acidity. Rioja Oriental drops to 300 m and turns Mediterranean, which is why Garnacha, not Tempranillo, is its traditional grape.

Three soils, three textures. Clay-calcareous on the Alavesa slopes gives the finest, most fragrant wines. Ferrous clay around Haro gives body and colour. Alluvial terraces by the river give lighter, earlier wines.
